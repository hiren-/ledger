Getting started

This tool scans an Indian equity universe (NSE + BSE, deduplicated, NSE preferred) and surfaces four kinds of insight:

  • Overview & category tabs — descriptive statistics of the whole universe and sortable tables by category.
  • Sector View — within-sector comparison, because valuation and margin norms vary wildly between industries.
  • Radar Scan — search by the five-factor composite shape.
  • Suggestions & Hidden Gems — consensus screens and asymmetry screens respectively.

Anywhere a table shows a row, click the row to open that stock's full detail card on the Symbol Search tab.

How to read the Composite Score Radar

The radar on the detail card plots five 0–100 scores as a pentagon. Each axis is a percentile-style composite built from a specific set of underlying metrics — not a single number. The shape of the pentagon is what matters, not the exact axis values.

The five axes

AxisWhat it measuresBuilt from
ValuationHow cheap the stock is relative to its earnings, book, sales, cash flow, and dividend.P/E, P/B, P/S, EV/EBITDA, P/FCF, earnings yield, FCF yield, dividend yield
QualityReturns on capital and margin structure — how good the business is.ROE, ROIC, ROA, gross/operating/net margins, cash conversion, extended F-score
GrowthDirection and speed of the business — how fast it's expanding.Revenue YoY, net income YoY, EBITDA YoY, gross profit YoY, FCF YoY, EPS YoY
Fin. HealthBalance-sheet robustness — can it survive a downturn?Debt/assets, net debt/EBITDA, current ratio, quick ratio, interest coverage, Altman Z
MomentumPrice action and technical opinion — is the market starting to notice?1M/3M/6M/1Y performance, aggregated technical rating
Reading rule of thumb: anything on an axis ≥ 70 is strong; 50–70 is decent; 30–50 is weak; < 30 is a red flag on that dimension. Values are relative to the scanned universe, so a "50" means the median Indian stock scores 50 on that axis.

The five common radar shapes

In practice, most stocks cluster into one of five recognisable pentagon shapes. Recognising the shape is faster than reading the five numbers individually.

All-Rounder
V 65 · Q 75 · G 65 · F 70 · M 60
A balanced pentagon. Strong everywhere, no weak axis. This is what most "quality compounders" look like. Easy to hold, rarely cheap. Best for: core positions. Watch for: nothing specific — the risk is just paying too much for a name everyone loves.
Growth-Dominant
V 30 · Q 70 · G 90 · F 60 · M 75
A spike toward Growth and Momentum. Expensive (Valuation often low), but the business is expanding fast and the market has noticed. Best for: momentum and growth mandates. Watch for: any slowdown — this shape de-rates violently.
Deep-Value
V 90 · Q 45 · G 30 · F 55 · M 25
A spike toward Valuation only. Cheap, but growth is slow, quality is mediocre, and the market has no interest. Classic value trap profile unless something else changes. Best for: contrarian, patience-driven positions. Watch for: the reason it's cheap.
Quality-First
V 55 · Q 90 · G 55 · F 85 · M 40
A spike toward Quality and Financial Health. Excellent business, rock-solid balance sheet, but not growing fast and not in favour. Best for: defensive, cash-flow-driven portfolios. Watch for: quality that's already been fully paid for.
Asymmetric
V 75 · Q 70 · G 75 · F 60 · M 30
A cheap-and-good-with-growth shape, but the market hasn't noticed yet (Momentum low). This is the rarest and arguably the most valuable pattern — mispriced quality. Best for: the highest-conviction long ideas. Watch for: Momentum never turning up — sometimes the market is right not to care.

What a healthy radar looks like

  • No axis below 30 unless you're deliberately running a specialist strategy (e.g. deep value means Valuation will dominate).
  • No axis above 90 with a low opposite axis — e.g. Growth 95 with Financial Health 25 usually means over-leveraged hypergrowth.
  • Shape should match your holding period: 6–12 month holds favour Momentum + Growth; 3–5 year holds favour Quality + Valuation.

Red flags in radar shapes

V 95 / Q 20 / F 20 — cheap and broken. Value trap. The valuation is low because the business is deteriorating.
M 95 / V 15 / Q 30 — pure price momentum, no fundamentals. Only valid for short-term trades with tight stops.
G 95 / F 30 — hypergrowth funded by debt. Works until rates rise or growth stalls.

Use the Radar Scan tab to search for a specific shape across the whole universe. The presets there correspond to the five shapes above plus a few others, and you can set your own thresholds on each axis.

The 5 Composite Scores + Overall

Each composite is a 0–100 weighted blend of underlying metrics. Scores are capped at 0 and 100. Where a metric is missing, the weight is renormalised across the available metrics — so a stock isn't penalised for a field TradingView doesn't expose.

Valuation Score 0–100
How cheap the stock is across multiple valuation lenses. Higher = cheaper.
  • P/E vs universe (25% weight)
  • EV/EBITDA (15%)
  • P/B (15%)
  • P/S (10%)
  • P/FCF (10%)
  • Earnings Yield (10%)
  • FCF Yield (10%)
  • Dividend Yield (5%)
≥ 70 = genuinely cheap; 50–70 = fair; < 30 = expensive.
Quality Score 0–100
Returns on capital, margin structure, and cash conversion — how good the business is.
  • ROE (20% weight)
  • ROIC (20%)
  • ROA (15%)
  • Gross margin (10%)
  • Operating margin (10%)
  • Net margin (10%)
  • Cash conversion (10%)
  • Extended F-Score (5%)
≥ 70 = high-quality business; < 40 = mediocre economics.
Growth Score 0–100
Direction and speed of revenue, earnings, and cash flow.
  • Revenue growth YoY (25%)
  • Net income growth YoY (25%)
  • EPS growth YoY (15%)
  • EBITDA growth YoY (15%)
  • Gross profit growth YoY (10%)
  • FCF growth YoY (10%)
≥ 70 = fast grower; 40–70 = steady; < 40 = flat or declining.
Financial Health Score 0–100
Balance-sheet robustness — leverage, liquidity, and bankruptcy risk.
  • Debt-to-assets (20%)
  • Net debt/EBITDA (20%)
  • Altman Z (20%)
  • Current ratio (15%)
  • Interest coverage (15%)
  • Quick ratio (10%)
≥ 70 = fortress balance sheet; < 30 = over-leveraged.
Momentum Score 0–100
Price action and technical opinion — is the market starting to move?
  • 1M performance (15%)
  • 3M performance (20%)
  • 6M performance (20%)
  • 1Y performance (20%)
  • Aggregated technical rating (25%)
≥ 70 = strong trend; < 30 = clearly out of favour.
Overall Score 0–100
Weighted blend of the five composites: Quality 30% · Valuation 20% · Growth 20% · Financial Health 15% · Momentum 15%. The 30% quality weight reflects the empirical fact that quality is the single most durable factor.
  • ≥ 70 = top of the universe
  • 55–70 = above average
  • 40–55 = middling
  • < 40 = below par

Piotroski F-Score & Extended F-Score

The Piotroski F-Score is a 9-point accounting quality check developed by Joseph Piotroski (2000). It measures whether a business is fundamentally improving, not just whether it's profitable today.

The 9 tests (each pass = 1 point)

  • Positive net income — the company made money.
  • Positive ROA — return on assets is positive.
  • Positive operating cash flow — real cash was generated.
  • CFO > Net Income — earnings are cash-backed, not accrual-driven.
  • Lower long-term debt YoY — leverage is being reduced.
  • Higher current ratio YoY — liquidity is improving.
  • No new shares issued — no dilution.
  • Higher gross margin YoY — pricing power or cost control.
  • Higher asset turnover YoY — using assets more efficiently.
Caveat in this app: TradingView exposes current-FY balance-sheet data reliably, but not always the prior-year raw line items needed for the change-based tests (5, 6, 7, 8, 9). Where the change can't be measured, we leave it as an explicit None rather than guessing. As a result, the F-Score here is conservative — a 7 is genuinely strong.
Extended F-Score 0–12
A heuristic approximation that uses the available current-period data (current ratio, D/E, gross margin, asset turnover) to estimate the leverage and efficiency points that can't be strictly verified. Directional, not certified. Use it as a sanity check on the strict F-Score.

Reading guide: 8–9 = exceptional; 7 = strong; 5–6 = mixed; 3–4 = weak; 0–2 = serious concern. Only stocks with a strict F-Score ≥ 7 appear in the "Piotroski Pass" list on the Suggestions tab.

Returns: ROE, ROA, ROIC, ROCE, DuPont

ROE — Return on Equity %
Net income / shareholders' equity. How much profit the company makes per rupee of shareholder capital. ≥ 15% is quality; ≥ 20% is excellent. Below 10% is weak. Watch out: ROE can be inflated by leverage, so always pair it with DuPont decomposition.
ROA — Return on Assets %
Net income / total assets. Profit per rupee of assets, regardless of funding. ≥ 8% is strong; ≥ 12% is exceptional. Lower-leverage-adjusted view than ROE.
ROIC — Return on Invested Capital %
After-tax operating profit / (debt + equity − cash). The cleanest measure of how well the company turns invested capital into profit. ≥ 15% is quality; ≥ 20% is exceptional. If ROIC > WACC consistently, the company creates value; if not, it destroys it.
ROCE — Return on Capital Employed %
EBIT / (total assets − current liabilities). Similar to ROIC but excludes cash treatment. Useful for capital-heavy businesses.
DuPont Decomposition 3-factor
Splits ROE into three multiplicative pieces: ROE = Net Margin × Asset Turnover × Equity Multiplier.
  • Net Margin — profitability per rupee of sales
  • Asset Turnover — sales per rupee of assets
  • Equity Multiplier — assets / equity (leverage)
This matters because a 25% ROE from Net Margin 15% × Asset Turnover 1.2 × Leverage 1.4 is a fundamentally better business than a 25% ROE from Net Margin 3% × Asset Turnover 0.8 × Leverage 10. Quality ROE: leverage < 2.0 with margin > 10%.

Valuation: P/E, P/B, P/S, PEG, Forward P/E

P/E — Price-to-Earnings ×
Price / earnings per share. The most-used valuation ratio. < 15 = cheap; 15–25 = fair; > 40 = expensive. But context matters — a 30× P/E for a 30% grower can be cheaper than a 15× P/E for a flat business. Always pair with PEG.
P/B — Price-to-Book ×
Price / book value per share. Measures how much you pay above accounting net worth. < 2 = reasonable for most sectors; < 1 = below book. Less useful for asset-light businesses (IT, pharma) where book value is tiny.
P/S — Price-to-Sales ×
Market cap / revenue. Useful when earnings are volatile or negative. < 2 = reasonable; < 1 = cheap. Sector-relative only — a 5× P/S is normal for software, abnormal for a distributor.
PEG — Price/Earnings-to-Growth ×
P/E divided by earnings growth rate. A PEG of 1 means you're paying 1× for 1% growth. < 1 = undervalued relative to growth; 1–2 = fair; > 2 = expensive. Only meaningful for growing companies.
Forward P/E ×
Price / next-year forecast EPS. If forward P/E < trailing P/E, the market expects earnings to grow. Useful check on whether a high trailing multiple is justified.
EV/EBITDA ×
Enterprise value / EBITDA. Capital-structure-neutral valuation. < 10 = cheap; 10–20 = fair; > 30 = expensive. Better cross-sector comparison than P/E.
Dividend Yield %
Annual dividend / price. 1.5–4% = sustainable yield zone; > 6% = often a warning. A very high yield usually means the price has fallen and the market expects a cut.

Risk models: Altman Z, Graham, NCAV

Altman Z-Score Z''
A bankruptcy-risk model originally developed by Edward Altman (1968). The version used here is the Z'' variant, adapted for emerging markets and non-manufacturers by removing the sales/assets term (which unfairly penalises asset-light businesses).

Formula: Z'' = 6.56 × X1 + 3.26 × X2 + 6.72 × X3 + 1.05 × X4
  • X1 = Working capital / Total assets
  • X2 = Retained earnings / Total assets (proxied by equity/assets)
  • X3 = EBIT / Total assets
  • X4 = Book equity / Total liabilities
Z > 2.6 = Safe zone · 1.1–2.6 = Grey zone · Z < 1.1 = Distress zone
Graham Number ₹
A rough intrinsic value estimate developed by Benjamin Graham: √(22.5 × EPS × BVPS). The 22.5 constant comes from a maximum P/E of 15 times a maximum P/B of 1.5.

Graham Margin of Safety = (Graham Number − Price) / Graham Number. MoS > 20% = meaningfully undervalued. Negative MoS = trading above Graham's fair value.

Only meaningful for profitable, asset-backed businesses — not applicable to asset-light or loss-making companies.
NCAV — Net Current Asset Value ₹
NCAV = Total current assets − Total liabilities. Graham's "net-net" test: buy only if Price / NCAV < 0.67 (buying for two-thirds of liquidation value).

Price / NCAV < 0.67 = deep net-net. 0.67–1.0 = attractive. > 1.0 = no margin of safety from the balance sheet.

Rare in modern markets, occasionally found in Indian micro-caps with thin analyst coverage.
Net Debt / EBITDA ×
Net debt divided by EBITDA. How many years of current cash flow it would take to repay net debt. < 2× = healthy. 2–4× = stretched. > 4× = high risk. Some sectors (utilities, telecom, infrastructure) run structurally higher — compare within sector.
Interest Coverage ×
EBIT / interest expense. How many times operating profit covers the interest bill. > 5× = comfortable. 2–5× = adequate. < 2× = dangerously thin. Below 1× means the company can't pay interest from operating profit.

Cash flow & quality: Accruals, Cash Conversion, FCF Yield

Cash is harder to manipulate than accounting profit. The metrics below are the ones professional forensic analysts look at first — and the ones most retail screens ignore entirely.

Accruals Ratio Sloan 1996
(Net income − Cash from operations) / Total assets. Measures how much of reported profit is not backed by cash.
  • Negative accruals = CFO > Net income. Reported profit understates cash generation. Strongly positive signal.
  • Near-zero accruals (0–0.02) = clean earnings.
  • Positive accruals > 0.05 = earnings are running ahead of cash. Warning.
  • > 0.15 = serious red flag. Historically one of the strongest predictors of future underperformance.
Target: < 0 (negative).
Cash Conversion CFO / NI
Operating cash flow divided by net income. > 1.0 means every rupee of reported profit is matched by at least a rupee of real cash. < 0.7 means earnings are largely accrual-driven and likely low-quality. Below 1.0 is a yellow flag; below 0.5 is a red flag.
FCF Yield %
Free cash flow / market cap. The inverse of P/FCF. > 7% = cheap on cash; > 10% = very cheap. Negative = the business is burning cash. FCF yield > earnings yield is a specific mispricing signal — the market is pricing accounting earnings higher than cash flow.
CFO Yield %
Operating cash flow / market cap. A broader cash-generating measure than FCF yield because it ignores capex. Useful when capex is lumpy.
FCF Margin %
Free cash flow / revenue. How much of each rupee of sales converts into free cash. > 12% = efficient cash conversion; > 20% = exceptional. < 5% = thin.

Technicals: Ratings, RSI, ADX, MACD, Bands, Crosses

TradingView Ratings -1 to +1
Three aggregated opinions from TradingView:
  • Tech Rating (Recommend.All) — overall, blending moving averages and oscillators
  • MA Rating — from the moving-average stack only
  • Oscillator Rating — from the oscillator stack only
Each maps to: Strong Buy (≥ 0.5) · Buy (0.1–0.5) · Neutral (−0.1–0.1) · Sell (−0.5–−0.1) · Strong Sell (≤ −0.5).
When all three read Strong Buy simultaneously, the trade is crowd-crowded. When none do, it's often a hidden-gem window.
RSI — Relative Strength Index 0–100
Momentum oscillator. > 70 = overbought (potential reversal or extension); < 30 = oversold (potential bounce). 45–65 with an upward slope is the "early uptrend" sweet spot — it's what the Quiet Inflection and Pre-Breakout archetypes look for.
ADX — Average Directional Index 0–100
Trend strength (not direction). < 20 = ranging / no trend. 20–25 = developing trend (the golden window for early entries). 25–40 = established trend. > 40 = very strong trend (often late). The +DI / −DI pair underneath tells you which direction the trend is going.
MACD momentum
Difference between 12-period and 26-period EMAs. A Bullish cross (MACD > signal) above zero is far more meaningful than one below zero — below-zero crosses are often just bear-market bounces.
Bollinger Bands volatility
Upper, lower, and basis (20-period SMA). Two derived metrics:
  • BB Position % — where price sits between lower (0%) and upper (100%). 35–75% = inside the band, not stretched.
  • BB Width % — band width / basis. When in the bottom 20% of the universe, it signals a volatility squeeze — the pre-breakout window.
Golden Cross / Death Cross trend
Golden Cross = fast MA crosses above slow MA (bullish). Two versions here:
  • GC 20/50 — SMA20 > SMA50. Fires early.
  • GC 50/200 — SMA50 > SMA200. Fires late, confirms the long-term trend.
The window between the two (20/50 fired, 50/200 not yet) is what the Pre-Breakout archetype targets — the crowd is still waiting for the slow cross while the setup is already live.
52W Range Position %
Where price sits between the 52-week low (0%) and 52-week high (100%). 55–80% = upper half, trend intact, not extended. > 90% = at highs, no margin of safety. < 20% = near lows, possible value or possible value trap.
Trend Alignment 0–4
Counts how many of {SMA20, SMA50, SMA100, SMA200} the price is above. 4/4 = full uptrend. 0/4 = full downtrend. 1–2 = early recovery.

Growth & trajectory: FY vs TTM

The single most under-used pattern in equity screens: comparing TTM growth to FY growth. When TTM > FY on multiple metrics, the business has inflected since the last reported annual — and the market almost always lags this discovery by 3–9 months.

FY growth annual
Growth rate over the last reported fiscal year (e.g. FY24 vs FY23). This is what every broker model is anchored on.
TTM growth trailing 12m
Growth rate over the last four reported quarters. This includes quarters that FY data doesn't yet reflect, so it picks up recent changes in trend much faster.
TTM > FY acceleration
When TTM growth is higher than FY growth, the trend is accelerating. Two or more of these together — EBITDA, FCF, Net Income, Gross Profit — is what the Quiet Inflection archetype targets.

3–4 metrics accelerating = strong signal. 1–2 = worth watching. 0 = no inflection.
QoQ growth quarterly
Sequential growth between consecutive quarters. Positive QoQ confirms the FY/TTM trend is still live. Negative QoQ is an early warning that the trajectory is rolling over.

The 12 Hidden Gem archetypes

Each archetype is a different way of finding asymmetry — a specific pattern that consensus screens miss. A stock can appear in multiple archetypes; the strongest divergences float to the top of each list.

1. Cash Compounder quality
Negative accruals + cash conversion ≥ 1.1 + ROE not leverage-driven + FCF yield > earnings yield. The signal: reported earnings understate real cash generation. Based on the Sloan (1996) accruals anomaly — one of the most robust accounting-based predictors in the literature. Why consensus misses it: accruals are not a headline number and require reading the cash flow statement.
2. Quiet Inflection growth
≥ 2 of {EBITDA, FCF, Net Income, Gross Profit} have TTM growth > FY growth, plus positive QoQ and RSI in the 45–68 band. The signal: the business has inflected since the last reported annual; sell-side models haven't caught up. Why consensus misses it: screens anchor on FY data.
3. Pre-Breakout technical
Golden Cross 20/50 fired + Golden Cross 50/200 not yet + ADX 18–28 with +DI > −DI + BB width in bottom 20% + RSI 45–65. The signal: the setup before consensus flips to "Strong Buy". Why consensus misses it: momentum screens demand the slow cross confirmation, by which time 30–40% of the move is gone.
4. Sector Orphan relative value
Sector composite > 70 + Overall score < 75 + Altman Safe + Graham MoS > 15%. The signal: best-in-class inside a sector the market dislikes. Why consensus misses it: sector-level narratives dominate stock-level analysis.
5. Forensic Clean forensic
Accruals < 0.02 + cash conversion ≥ 0.9 + no receivables/inventory bloat + leverage contained + goodwill < 15% of assets. The signal: the books survive Beneish M-Score and Montier C-Score scrutiny. Why consensus misses it: forensic accounting is not part of normal retail screens.
6. FCF King cash yield
FCF yield > 7% + FCF margin > 12% + CFO yield > 10% + net debt/EBITDA < 2 + cash conversion > 1.0. The signal: cash flowing in faster than the market is pricing. Why consensus misses it: most screens look at P/E and ignore cash yields.
7. Asset-Light Compounder ROIC
ROIC > 18% + D/E < 0.5 + operating margin > 15% + FCF margin > 10%. The signal: high returns without ballooning the balance sheet. Based on the asset growth anomaly (Fama-French CMA, q-factor). Why consensus misses it: the anomaly is academic and not in retail screens.
8. Graham Net-Net deep value
Price / NCAV < 1.0 (ideally < 0.67) + current ratio > 1.2 + Altman not Distress. The signal: trading below liquidation value. Why consensus misses it: rare, micro-cap, no analyst coverage.
9. Momentum Ignition CANSLIM
6M performance > 10% + 52W range position > 55% + TTM growth positive + EPS QoQ > 0 + forward P/E < trailing P/E. The signal: price and fundamental momentum aligning. Why consensus misses it: requires both technical and fundamental screens simultaneously.
10. Buffett Quality quality
ROE > 18% + ROIC > 15% + D/E < 0.5 + interest coverage > 8× + net debt/EBITDA < 1× + positive FCF + DuPont leverage < 2. The signal: the durable cash-generative profile Buffett favours. Why consensus misses it: the combination of low debt and high returns is uncommon and often ignored in favour of pure growth.
11. Dividend Growth income
Yield 1.5–5% + DPS growth YoY > 8% + payout < 60% + FCF yield > 4% + ROE > 14%. The signal: a growing, cash-backed, sustainable dividend. Why consensus misses it: yield screens chase the highest yield, which usually signals distress.
12. Working Capital Wizard operations
Receivables/assets < 20% + inventory/assets < 25% + current ratio 1.3–4 + cash conversion > 1.0 + ROIC > 14%. The signal: operational discipline visible in working capital. Based on Abarbanell & Bushee (1998). Why consensus misses it: working capital ratios are not on standard screens.

Trap checks & red flags

Every gem card shows a "Trap check" row. This is a quick validity check on the underlying thesis.

Altman

Zone reading: Safe (≥ 2.6), Grey (1.1–2.6), Distress (< 1.1). Distress invalidates most archetypes.

Int.Cov — Interest Coverage

≥ 3× is healthy. < 2× is a red flag. Below 1× means interest is not covered from operating profit.

NetDebt/EBITDA

< 2× is comfortable. > 4× is stretched.

Accruals

< 0 is best (cash-backed earnings). > 0.05 is warning. > 0.15 is serious.

CashConv — Cash Conversion

≥ 1.0 = every rupee of profit backed by cash. < 0.7 = accrual-driven.

Universal red flags

  • All three ratings Strong Buy — crowded trade, not a gem. Hidden Gem archetypes auto-exclude these (Momentum Ignition excepted).
  • 52W range position > 90% — no margin of safety.
  • Altman Distress — structurally risky; most archetypes auto-exclude.
  • Accruals > 0.10 with Cash Conversion < 0.7 — earnings quality is likely poor.
  • RSI > 75 in a Pre-Breakout or Inflection candidate — you're late.
